A screenplay becomes a rough cut. Rocky (1976), scenes 1–8.

1 · Parse the screenplay

runs live

Reads the PDF and re-derives the beat list on every call — nothing here is replayed. Takes about a minute.

2 · Render the cut

The same scenes at three states. Assembly is live; the panels, voices and motion it assembles were generated ahead of time.

    Today’s generation budget

    —

    Google caps each model at 100 requests a day. Three of the controls below cost nothing; one of them can spend a third of the image budget in a click.

    3 · Swap a shot for real footage

    0% real

    Drop a clip on any shot. The filename does not matter — the beat it lands on does. Remove it and the drawn shot comes back.

    4 · Characters

    —

    Point a character at a folder of reference images and its panels redraw from that art instead of being re-invented from text each time. The number on each row is what the redraw costs.

    5 · Edit a shot

    free unless you redraw

    Pick a shot, then hold it longer, describe a change, or drop in a version you edited yourself. Only the described change costs a call.

    6 · Dailies

    free

    A take covering a range of beats. It plays its own full length with its own production sound, so the cut gets shorter or longer to fit it.
